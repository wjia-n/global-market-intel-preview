Platform 1 · How it connects

From foreign site to indexable static page

1 · FreshRSS

Run in Docker. Stores, sorts and manages feeds and users. Built-in XPath rules scrape sites with no RSS feed.

2 · Newspaper4k cron

A Python cron job checks your foreign business URL list, downloads new pages and extracts clean article text — no sidebars, ads or navbars.

3 · LibreTranslate

The raw string is sent to your self-hosted LibreTranslate server with a local POST request. Free, 50+ languages, no per-character charge.

4 · Database

Translated text, sector, region, sentiment and source are saved as one structured record, ready to filter and export.

5 · Astro API → static

Astro reads the API at build time and generates fast, indexable static pages — the format AdSense and search engines prefer.

Cron + Newspaper4k + LibreTranslate (Python, sketch)

from newspaper import Article
import requests

article = Article(url)  # German, Chinese or Brazilian trade portal
article.download(); article.parse()
clean_text = article.text  # body only: no sidebar, ads or nav

payload = dict(q=clean_text, source="auto", target="en")
res = requests.post("http://localhost:5000/translate", json=payload)
translated = res.json()["translatedText"]
# Save translated + sector + region + sentiment to your database.

Start the pieces (local / Docker)

# FreshRSS (Docker)
docker run -d --name freshrss -p 8080:80 freshrss/freshrss
# LibreTranslate (Docker, local only)
docker run -d --name libretranslate -p 5000:5000 libretranslate/libretranslate
# Crawler: run the Python script on a cron schedule,
# e.g. every 30 minutes, against your source URL list.

Keep the translation server on localhost or your own server. Do not send corporate reading material to a paid third-party translator when the free self-hosted route covers the language.

How it works · Dashboard overview

How the Global Market Intel Scraper works

Global Market Intel Scraper is an international business news tracker built for managers who cannot wait for translated headlines to travel through agencies, wire summaries, or social feeds. It watches public corporate press pages, trade journals, government portals, and port bulletins across manufacturing, semiconductor, tariff, and logistics hubs, then turns them into one searchable B2B market intelligence dashboard. Instead of opening dozens of foreign-language sites each morning, a supply chain manager opens one stream, filters by sector, sentiment, and source region, and reads the developments that change purchasing, shipping, and compliance decisions.

The engine behind the dashboard is algorithmic crawling, not manual curation. A cron job checks a trusted source list for each region and sector, fetches pages at set intervals, and records new or changed articles. FreshRSS, run in Docker, stores, sorts, and manages these feeds, and its XPath scraping rules find article links on sites without a usable feed. Each candidate URL is then passed to a parsing stage modelled on the open-source Python Newspaper4k library. Newspaper4k downloads the page, identifies the main article body, and extracts the title, author, publication date, header image, and clean text, while ignoring sidebars, navigation menus, advertising blocks, related-article widgets, and comment sections. Parsing HTML metadata without sidebars matters because corporate pages are dense with boilerplate, and a single sidebar paragraph about an unrelated product can pollute a sentiment score or a keyword search. By keeping only the article body and its structured metadata, the platform preserves the signal and discards the chrome around it.

Once text is extracted, it enters the translation pipeline. The platform runs open-source translation locally, rather than sending corporate reading material to a third-party service. A self-hosted LibreTranslate instance handles 50+ languages, while Meta's NLLB-200 model provides broader coverage across two hundred languages. The raw string is sent to LibreTranslate with a local POST request, then the translated text is saved to the database. Managing open-source translation models like Meta's NLLB-200 means choosing a model size that fits available hardware, caching repeated phrases, and adding a glossary for corporate and legal jargon, so terms such as tariff schedule, bill of lading, force majeure, and dual-use classification translate consistently every time. The automated translation global news portal shows the raw text beside the clean English conversion, so a bilingual reader can check any doubtful sentence against its source before acting.

Filtering turns this translated archive into a working tool for real time commodity tracking and trade tariff regulatory monitoring. Articles carry structured sector, region, date, and sentiment fields, covering Logistics, Agritech, Supply Chain, and Raw Commodities, marked Bullish, Bearish, or Volatile. A procurement lead can isolate volatile raw commodity updates from Brazil and Japan, a logistics manager can follow port news from China and Germany, and a compliance officer can watch tariff and regulatory notices across all regions at once. Because the data is structured, the same filtered view can be exported as clean CSV or JSON for corporate reporting, shared with a team, or exposed via an API that Astro reads to generate fast, indexable static pages, making this a free cross border market scraper interface in spirit and a foreign corporate news aggregator in practice.

Global Market Intel Scraper is therefore positioned as a global supply chain news utility first and a news reader second. It does not replace legal advice, brokerage research, or official customs guidance, and it does not claim live pricing or guaranteed accuracy for translated text. It gives readers faster access to international developments that affect costs, lead times, and regulatory risk, with the source, date, and original wording one click away.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How can businesses track international regulatory shifts automatically? +

Set a crawler to watch official government portals, trade-ministry notices and regulator press pages on a schedule. The scraper parses each new notice, extracts its date, jurisdiction and subject, and files it under a compliance tag. A filtered stream — by region, sector or keyword — then surfaces only the notices that change duties, reporting dates or import rules, instead of the full firehose of government publishing.

What is an open source news scraper engine? +

An open source news scraper engine is software that fetches a public web page, reads its HTML structure, and extracts the article body, title, author, publication date and images while ignoring navigation, sidebars and advertising. Tools such as Newspaper4k do this in Python with multithreading, so one scheduled job can process hundreds of corporate press pages and store clean, structured records in a database for search and filtering.

How does machine translation handle specific corporate and legal jargon? +

General translation models struggle with jargon, so a production pipeline adds a glossary layer: terms such as tariff schedule, bill of lading, force majeure and dual-use classification are mapped to approved translations before the text is published. Meta's NLLB-200 model handles the broad language transfer across 200 languages, and the glossary keeps company, legal and commodity terms consistent from one article to the next.

Why use an independent web scraper instead of an RSS reader feed? +

RSS readers only show what a publisher chooses to syndicate, and many corporate and government sites publish no feed at all. An independent scraper reads the page itself, so it can cover press pages, regulator portals and regional journals that never appear in a feed, apply its own filters and sentiment scores, and keep a searchable archive even after the original page changes.

How to filter global commodities updates by financial sentiment? +

First tag each article with a sector and a region, then score its language: rising prices, tight supply and strong demand lean bullish; falling prices, weak orders and surplus lean bearish; sharp swings, conflicting signals and policy uncertainty lean volatile. On this dashboard, choose a sector such as Raw Commodities, then a sentiment, and the stream narrows to matching updates — which you can export as CSV or JSON for a morning report.

Can I stream translated B2B headlines directly into a corporate workspace app? +

Yes. Once headlines are cleaned, translated and stored as structured records, they can be exposed through a simple JSON feed or API that workspace tools, chat apps and internal dashboards can poll. Because each record carries a stable sector, region, sentiment and source, the receiving app can filter the stream itself and post only the items a given team — logistics, procurement or compliance — actually needs.
